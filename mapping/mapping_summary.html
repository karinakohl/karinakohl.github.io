Key findings

  • Nine activity categories: capstones and academic team projects account for 22 of the 40 studies, serious games for 7, and the six remaining categories for 11.
  • Evidence is mostly perception-based: 26 studies measure only students’ post-intervention opinions, 6 are reports without student data, and 8 have some comparative design.
  • The studies cover 18 of the 40 SeSSBOK skills. Collaboration appears in 38 studies, Problem Awareness & Solving in 18, Personal Competencies in 17.
  • In breadth, Personal Competencies is the narrowest domain (4 of 13 skills, 31%), against 57% for Problem Awareness & Solving and 50% for Collaboration.
  • The Evaluation and Cooperation subdomains are not cited at all; Teamwork is cited only generically (31 studies).
  • 78 of 198 soft-skill mentions (39%) use generic labels such as “communication” or “teamwork”.
  • Teaching aligns with the top market demands (teamwork, communication) but does not name adaptive skills — flexibility, change management, learning — nor listening.
